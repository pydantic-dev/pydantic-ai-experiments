03 · Tools & capabilities

A tool can fail in three ways, and each one means something different to the run.

raise ModelRetry('…')

“Fix your arguments”

The message goes back to the model and it tries again. Uses the tool’s retry budget; when it runs out, UnexpectedModelBehavior.

raise ToolFailed('…')

“Done, but it failed”

A definitive result such as “file not found”. Uses no retry budget, so cap repeats with UsageLimits.

any other exception

“Stop everything”

Aborts the run, unless a hook or ReportToolErrors turns it into one of the other two.

Pydantic AI Field Guide · Tools · docs/tools-advanced.md#tool-retries

This is one of the most useful distinctions to internalise. ModelRetry is for problems the model can fix, such as a malformed path, a missing full name or an invalid filter; it appears in history as a tool return with outcome='retried'. ToolFailed is new in version 2 and is for outcomes that are final, such as a 404 or a permission denied; it appears with outcome='failed' and costs no retry budget. Anything else propagates and ends the run, which is right for bugs but harsh for flaky dependencies. You can convert exceptions with an on_tool_execute_error hook, or use the Harness ReportToolErrors capability. Retry budget precedence: per-tool, then toolset, then override, then run(retries=), then Agent(retries=), then 1. args_validator(ctx, params) runs after schema validation and before execution or approval, which is the right place for business rules you want checked before a human is asked.
